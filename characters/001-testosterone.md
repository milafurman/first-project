# EPISODE 001 — TESTOSTERONE

**Character:** Sir Osterone, the Blacksmith Knight
**Villain:** The Fade
**Protagonist:** Queen Meridia (the body)

---

## THE CHARACTER — SIR OSTERONE

A knight who is also a blacksmith — because testosterone doesn't just fight, it *builds*.

- **Origin:** Forged in Castle Cholesterol, of House Steroid. (Testosterone is a steroid hormone synthesized from cholesterol — see Science Appendix, S1.)
- **How he's summoned:** He never acts on his own. Ravens fly from the Pituitary Tower carrying royal letters sealed in wax — and only when a letter arrives does Sir Osterone ride. (LH signaling from the anterior pituitary — S2.)
- **What he does when he arrives:** He doesn't smash things. He walks into the castle library, pulls the old books off the shelves, and *rewrites the building plans*. Then the castle builds itself stronger. (Androgen receptor → gene transcription → protein synthesis — S3.)
- **His works:**
  - Hammers muscle onto the castle walls (muscle protein synthesis — S4)
  - Keeps the towers dense and unshakeable (bone density — S5)
  - Stokes the Queen's fireplace — *and we shall say no more about the Queen's fireplace* (libido — S6)
  - Feeds the red river that runs beneath the kingdom (erythropoiesis — S7)
- **Personality:** Gruff, loyal, criminally underappreciated. Everyone assumes he only serves kings. He's been in the Queen's service the whole time and he's a little tired of the rumor.
- **The myth-bust (episode one's whole reason to exist):** In the Queen's court, Sir Osterone outnumbers Lady Estradiol. By a lot. (In women, circulating testosterone concentrations exceed estradiol concentrations — S8.)

## THE VILLAIN — THE FADE

A gray mist that rolls down from the mountains. Not a monster. Worse: a *tax collector*.

- **Origin story (long version):** The Fade was never born — it simply started collecting. It doesn't storm the gates. It skims. One percent of the kingdom's fire, every year, starting in the kingdom's fourth decade. So quiet nobody files a complaint. (Age-related testosterone decline of roughly 1–2% per year in men from the 30s onward; in women, testosterone falls steeply through the reproductive years — mid-40s levels are about half of what they were in the 20s — S9, S10.)
- **Visual (creative integration beat — the ONE allowed metaphor-touch):** The Fade rolls over Queen Meridia's shoulders as gray mist while she sits on her throne. Colors desaturate around her. Her crown literally gets heavier. NO diagrams. NO cross-sections. NO cell animations in the notes. The mist on her shoulders is the entire visual language.
- **What The Fade takes:** the forge-fire (energy), the wall-building (muscle), the fireplace (you know), the spark in the Queen's eye (mood).

## THE PROTAGONIST — QUEEN MERIDIA

The body. A queen in her prime who built this kingdom herself and has zero intention of dimming.

- She is never weak, never at fault, never the problem. The kingdom is glorious. The Fade is a thief.
- Her arc in episode one: she notices the mist, everyone tells her "that's just what happens to kingdoms," and she says the series' thesis line: **"Nothing 'just happens' in my kingdom."**

## LONG VERSION — YouTube (outline)

**Act I — The Kingdom (0:00–1:30):** Queen Meridia's kingdom at full blaze. We meet Sir Osterone at his forge, forged himself in Castle Cholesterol, working only when the ravens come from the Pituitary Tower. Running joke: everyone at court thinks he works for the neighboring King. He does not correct them anymore. He just sighs and hammers.

**Act II — The Fade's Origin (1:30–3:00):** The Fade introduced not with thunder but with paperwork. It has always collected its quiet tax from every kingdom — starting around the fourth decade, one percent a year, never enough in any single year to notice. Montage: fires slightly dimmer each season. The court normalizes it. "Kingdoms cool. It's natural." The Fade's mist rolls over the Queen's shoulders on her throne (the integration beat — mist on shoulders, nothing anatomical, ever).

**Act III — The Summoning (3:00–4:30):** The Queen goes looking for the blacksmith everyone forgot she employed. Finds the forge cold, ravens grounded. She writes the letter herself. Emotional beat: she doesn't beg him to save her — she *re-commissions* him. She was never broken; she was under-supplied.

**Act IV — Sir Osterone's Monologue (4:30–5:30):**
> "Everyone thinks I'm a king's man. Swords, beards, chest-thumping — that's the rumor. But I was forged for building, not brawling. I rewrite the plans in the library and the walls rebuild themselves. I've served this Queen since before she wore the crown — her towers, her fire, her *fireplace* — and I never once asked for a statue. All I ever needed was the letter. Send the raven, Your Majesty. The forge remembers."

**Act V — The Kingdom Rekindled (5:30–6:30):** Fires up, walls thick, Fade retreating to the mountains — not destroyed (it never is; it's entropy) but *held at the border*. Closing thesis + logo beat.

## COMPLIANCE GUARDRAILS (Meta ad review / TikTok)

- The scripts personify physiology; they never claim a product treats, cures, or prevents anything. "The Fade can be fought" is narrative; the appendix carries the evidence, scoped to what the citations actually support.
- No dosing, no protocols, no "ask your doctor for X" — nothing that reads as drug promotion.
- Innuendo stays at "the Queen's fireplace" altitude: deniable, wink-level, zero explicit language. Kids hear a fireplace. Adults hear a fireplace. *Everyone understands the fireplace.*
- No before/after body imagery implied in animation notes (Meta personal-health policy trigger).
