## STORY 2 — "Retatrutide and the Three Hungry Dragons"

> Once upon a time, in a kingdom called the Body, there lived a princess named Bella. Bella was
> beautiful, but she was *starving* — not for food, but for *control*. Three dragons circled her
> castle: the GLP-1 dragon, the GIP dragon, and the glucagon dragon. They didn't breathe fire.
> They breathed *cravings*. Every time Bella tried to eat a salad, the dragons whispered,
> "Cake. Cake. Cake."
>
> The kingdom's old witch — Metabolism — used to keep the dragons in check with her potions.
> But the witch was aging. Her potions were weak. The dragons grew bold. Bella's waistline
> expanded. Her energy collapsed. She stopped dancing. She stopped riding. She just... sat on
> the throne and ate.
>
> Then Retatrutide rode in. He was a slick, cocky little shit — a triple-threat knight who hit
> all three dragons at once. He didn't fight them. He *bored* them. "You want cake?" he said.
> "Eat the salad. You'll feel better." The dragons blinked. They'd never heard that before.
> They shrugged, flew off, and Bella's kingdom was hers again.
>
> Voiceover: "One drug. Three receptors. Zero fucks given."

### HEAD WRITER NOTES — Story 2

- **GOLDEN RULE VIOLATION (must fix):** GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon are the body's own hormones —
  naming the dragons after them makes parts of the body the villains. The villain must be the
  *process*: dysregulated appetite signaling. Fix: the dragons are "Craving," "Storage," and
  "the Empty Furnace" (or similar process-names), and retatrutide is the knight who speaks all
  three dragon languages — because that's the actual mechanism.
- **SCIENCE FLAG (backwards mechanism):** Retatrutide is an *agonist* at all three receptors —
  it activates the GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors; it doesn't silence or bore the hormones.
  GLP-1 receptor agonism *reduces* appetite. As drafted, the dragons do what the drug does.
  The "speaks their language / gives the orders the aging witch no longer sends" framing fixes
  story and science in one move. Anchor citation available: Jastreboff AM, et al., retatrutide
  phase 2 obesity trial, *N Engl J Med.* 2023;389:514–526.
- **SERIES CONTINUITY (see Story 5):** GLP-1 cannot be a villain dragon here and a hero monk in
  Story 5. One canon. Resolve before either episode locks.
- **COMPLIANCE:** "cocky little shit" and "Zero fucks given" break the series' own PG-13 /
  Meta-ad-review ceiling (double entendres only, no profanity). Keep the swagger, cut the words —
  e.g. VO: "One knight. Three dragons. Not a single drop of sweat."
- **What's working:** Bella starving for *control*, not food — that's the emotional spine of the
  whole GLP-1-era conversation and it should survive every rewrite. "Cake. Cake. Cake." is a
  keeper. Retatrutide boring the dragons instead of fighting them is genuinely funny and close
  enough to "satiety signaling" to survive a reframe.

## STORY 3 — "BPC-157 and the Broken Knight"

> Sir Galahad fell off his horse. Not dramatically — just... slipped. His shoulder popped out of
> its socket like a bad relationship. The kingdom's healers tried everything: bandages, prayers,
> a questionable poultice made of goat urine. Nothing worked. Galahad sat in the infirmary, arm
> in a sling, watching his life pass him by.
>
> Then BPC-157 showed up. A scrappy little repairman with a toolbelt and a permanent scowl.
> "I don't do miracles," he said. "I do *repairs*." He crawled into the wound like a plumber
> into a pipe, welded the tendon back together, and patched the inflammation like it was a leaky
> faucet.
>
> Galahad stood. The arm worked. He rode off into the sunset, flipping off the healers who'd
> given up on him.
>
> Voiceover: "When the body breaks, BPC-157 fixes it. No prayers. No goat urine. Just repair."

### HEAD WRITER NOTES — Story 3

- **SCIENCE FLAG (the big one):** BPC-157's evidence base is almost entirely rodent studies —
  tendon, ligament, and gut healing in rats. There are no published human RCTs to cite, which
  collides head-on with the series rule "cite your sources — no vibes." Options: (a) write the
  episode honestly as "the repairman with a legend bigger than his paperwork" — lean into the
  folk-tale framing, appendix cites the animal literature as animal literature (e.g. Sikiric P,
  et al., *Curr Pharm Des.* 2018;24(18):1990–2001, review of preclinical data); or (b) hold the
  episode until the evidence matures. Recommend (a) — the fairy-tale format is uniquely suited
  to "legend vs. ledger" honesty, and it inoculates against comment-section debunkers.
- **COMPLIANCE:** "flipping off the healers" — gesture reads as explicit on Meta review; swap
  for a wordless tip of the visor. Also FDA has flagged BPC-157 on its compounding lists;
  voiceover "When the body breaks, BPC-157 fixes it" is an efficacy claim — soften to the
  repairman's own line, "I don't do miracles. I do repairs," which is already better copy.
- **What's working:** The repairman-with-a-scowl characterization is perfect. Goat urine poultice
  is peak Shrek-elevated. "Popped out of its socket like a bad relationship" — lock it.

## STORY 4 — "NAD+ and the Dim Princess"

> Princess Aurora fell asleep. Not the fairy-tale kind — the *real* kind. Her cells were running
> out of NAD+, the kingdom's energy currency. Without it, her mitochondria — the little power
> plants — sputtered and died. Aurora's skin dulled. Her hair thinned. She forgot her own name.
>
> The kingdom's old alchemist — Sirtuin — tried to help, but he needed NAD+ to function, and
> there was none left. It was a chicken-and-egg problem from hell.
>
> Then NAD+ showed up. A glowing, ancient wizard with a staff made of pure energy. "I've been
> here all along," he said. "You just stopped making me." He flooded the cells with fresh NAD+,
> the mitochondria roared back to life, and Aurora woke up — not just awake, but *sharp*. She
> remembered her name. She remembered her kingdom. She remembered how to dance.
>
> Voiceover: "NAD+ is the spark. Without it, everything goes dark. With it, the princess wakes up."

### HEAD WRITER NOTES — Story 4

- **GOLDEN RULE CHECK (passes, barely):** The villain is implicit — age-related NAD+ decline.
  Give it a name and a face (candidate: "The Dimming") so the villain-is-a-process rule is
  visible on screen, not just in subtext. The Sleeping Beauty inversion is strong.
- **CHARACTER LOGIC FLAG:** NAD+ is both the depleted resource AND the arriving hero — the
  character says "you stopped making me," then floods the cells with himself. Cleaner: the
  wizard returns via his apprentices (the precursors — NR/NMN in the appendix), or he's been
  locked out of the kingdom and the episode is about reopening the gates (salvage pathway).
  Pick one metaphor and hold it.
- **SCIENCE FLAG (moderate):** NAD+ decline with age and the sirtuin dependency are solid
  (Covarrubias AJ, et al., *Nat Rev Mol Cell Biol.* 2021;22:119–141). But human outcome data for
  NAD+ boosters (energy, cognition, "sharpness") is thin — mostly biomarker studies showing
  precursors raise NAD+ levels, not that anyone remembered how to dance. Voiceover overclaims;
  appendix must scope it. "Mitochondria sputtered and died" also overstates — impaired, not dead.
- **ANIMATION NOTE — WATCH THE CHEAT:** "mitochondria, the little power plants" is exactly the
  diagram-creep the series bible bans. Power plants stay *story-world* power plants: furnaces in
  the castle basement, never organelles on screen. The mist gets whoever draws a mitochondrion.
- **What's working:** Chicken-and-egg problem from hell is a great villain mechanic. "She forgot
  her own name" → "She remembered her name" is a beautiful arc frame.

## STORY 5 — "GLP-1 and the Bottomless Pit"

> King Henry had a bottomless pit in his stomach. Not a metaphor — an actual pit. Every meal he
> ate, the pit swallowed it whole and demanded more. The kingdom's farmers worked overtime. The
> bakers collapsed. Henry gained weight, lost energy, and started blaming the pit. "It's not my
> fault," he said. "The pit made me do it."
>
> The pit laughed. It was a *process*, not a person. It didn't care about Henry's feelings. It
> just wanted food.
>
> Then GLP-1 showed up. A calm, steady monk who didn't raise his voice. "The pit isn't the
> problem," he said. "Your *response* to it is." He taught Henry to listen to the pit without
> obeying it. Henry ate less. The pit shrank. Henry's energy returned. He started riding again.
>
> Voiceover: "GLP-1 doesn't kill the pit. It teaches you to stop feeding it."

### HEAD WRITER NOTES — Story 5

- **SERIES CONTINUITY (RESOLVED in Episode 002):** The Story 2 draft made GLP-1 a villain
  dragon while this story makes it a hero monk. Episode 002 (`002-retatrutide.md`) resolved it:
  the dragons are process-villains (Craving, the Hoard, the Cold Furnace) and GLP-1 is
  canonically heroic. The monk is cleared for this episode — and Sir Reta is now canonically
  "the knight who speaks the monk's language and two more besides."
- **GOLDEN RULE CHECK (passes):** "It was a *process*, not a person" — this line IS the series
  thesis said out loud. Keep it forever.
- **GOLDEN-RULE EDGE:** "The pit isn't the problem, your response is" drifts toward blaming
  Henry (the body). Nudge: the pit's *messengers got louder over the years* and the monk quiets
  the messengers — appetite signaling, not willpower. Keeps Henry blameless per the bible.
- **SCIENCE FLAG (minor):** Mechanistically GLP-1 agonists slow gastric emptying and act on
  hypothalamic/brainstem appetite circuits — "listen to the pit without obeying it" is actually
  a fair poetic rendering of increased satiety. Citable: Drucker DJ, "Mechanisms of Action and
  Therapeutic Application of Glucagon-like Peptide-1," *Cell Metab.* 2018;27(4):740–756.
  "The pit shrank" — fine as metaphor; appendix should note weight loss, not literal gastric
  shrinkage.
- **What's working:** The monk characterization is the best character sketch in the slate.
  Calm hero vs. loud process is a fresh dynamic after two knights.

## STORY 6 — "Ipamorelin and the Shrinking Knight"

> Sir Lancelot was shrinking. Not in height — in *mass*. His muscles were wasting away like a
> snowman in July. The kingdom's trainers tried protein shakes, heavy lifting, even a cursed
> amulet that promised "gains." Nothing stuck.
>
> Then Ipamorelin showed up. A quiet, intense little growth hormone secretagogue with a
> single-minded focus: *pulse*. "You don't need more growth hormone," he said. "You need
> *pulses* of it." He triggered natural pulses of GH, which told the muscles to grow, not just
> maintain.
>
> Lancelot bulked up. His armor fit again. He challenged the old trainer to a duel and won —
> not because he was stronger, but because he was *recovered*.
>
> Voiceover: "Ipamorelin pulses. The body responds. The knight grows."

### HEAD WRITER NOTES — Story 6

- **CHARACTER NOTE:** "A quiet, intense little growth hormone secretagogue" — the mechanism name
  can't be the character description on screen (that's the diagram-cheat in verbal form). Give
  him a storybook identity: a bell-ringer or drummer — a character whose whole power is *rhythm*.
  "It's not how loud you ring the bell. It's *when*." That's pulsatility, in-world.
- **SCIENCE FLAG (moderate):** Ipamorelin is a selective ghrelin-receptor agonist (GHS-R) that
  stimulates pulsatile GH release with minimal cortisol/prolactin effect — that selectivity is
  its actual claim to fame and worth a beat (Raun K, et al., "Ipamorelin, the first selective
  growth hormone secretagogue," *Eur J Endocrinol.* 1998;139(5):552–561). But human clinical
  outcome data (muscle mass, recovery) is scarce — mostly early-phase and preclinical. "Lancelot
  bulked up" is the GH-axis story, not an ipamorelin trial result; appendix must say so.
  Note: not FDA-approved; compounding-pharmacy gray zone — same compliance posture as BPC-157.
- **GOLDEN RULE CHECK (needs a villain):** No named villain — muscle wasting just "happens."
  Give the process a face: "The Wither" or fold it into the Fog of Decline's family (the Fog of Decline already
  established in Episode 001 as the entropy-tax collector; sarcopenia is a natural second
  business line for the same villain — series-arc opportunity).
- **What's working:** "Wasting away like a snowman in July." The cursed amulet that promised
  "gains" — supplement-industry satire the audience will feel seen by. "Not stronger —
  *recovered*" is a killer closing distinction and quietly accurate.

3. **Evidence-tier labeling:** BPC-157 and ipamorelin are preclinical/gray-market; retatrutide is
   investigational (phase 3); GLP-1 agonists and testosterone are approved medicines with human
   RCTs. Decide whether episodes visually signal the tier (e.g. the character's "royal charter"
   — full charter, provisional charter, folk legend) so the series' credibility survives scrutiny.
