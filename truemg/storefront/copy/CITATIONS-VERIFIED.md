# Citations — the verified set

Every reference below was pulled from **PubMed** and then re-checked by me against PubMed's
own metadata record: exact title, journal, year, authors, DOI. Nothing here came from memory,
and nothing a search merely suggested was kept without that second check.

## The two rules I applied, and what they cost

**Rule 1 — identity and method only.** A paper qualifies if its title describes *the molecule*
or *the measurement*. It does not qualify if the title describes *an effect*.

**Rule 2 — no "doping", no "performance".** "Performance" is a banned word on this site, and a
reference list where half the entries say *doping control* tells a reader we expect athletes.
The anti-doping labs genuinely are the best peptide analysts in the world, so this rule threw
away good science — but it threw away the wrong impression with it.

## Attribution

PubMed requires that material drawn from it is credited and that DOIs appear as links. The
page does both: a line crediting PubMed, and every DOI rendered as a full `https://doi.org/…`
link.

## Group C, and the five that ship with nothing

The last search group returned SS-31, MOTS-c, Epithalon, Semax and Selank. Three of the five
were rejected on Rule 1 after I checked them:

- **SS-31** — the standard primary citation (Zhao 2004, PMID 15178689) is titled for what the
  peptide *does*: "…inhibit mitochondrial swelling, oxidative cell death, and reperfusion
  injury". The one paper that does report HPLC/MS purity for SS-31 itself, PMID 34056004, is
  tagged **Retracted Publication** in PubMed. Confirmed against the record myself. It must
  never be cited.
- **MOTS-c** — the discovery paper (Lee 2015, Cell Metab, PMID 25738459) is titled "…promotes
  metabolic homeostasis and reduces obesity and insulin resistance". The only analytical paper
  is doping-framed. Nothing survives.
- **Selank** — no identity paper exists in PubMed; the one analytical paper is Russian-language
  with no DOI and does not name Selank in its title, so a reader could not check it.

Kept: **Epithalon** (Khavinson 2017, PMID 29124531 — "Identification of Peptide AEDG in the
Polypeptide Complex of the Pineal Gland", a genuine HPLC/MS characterization) and **Semax**
(Shevchenko 2013, PMID 23652441 — "Stability of Semax acetyl to proteolysis in various
biological media"). Rejected for Epithalon: PMID 25535022, whose real title contains "assumed
to be a potential treatment for cancer, old age and Retinitis Pigmentosa".

## What Lapis itself refused, which was the surprise

The first attempt to publish was **rejected by the platform's compliance filter**, not by me:

> Offending text: aboutBody: "Therapeutics", "Drug", "titration".

All three were inside material I cannot rewrite — a paper title, a journal name, and a method
name:

| Word | Where it actually came from |
|---|---|
| Therapeutics | D'Addio's title, *…Characterization of Peptide Therapeutics* |
| Drug | the journal name *Drug Test Anal* |
| titration | *isothermal titration calorimetry*, read by the filter as dosing language |

**The lesson worth keeping:** on this platform a citation is not exempt from the copy filter.
A reference whose title or journal contains a flagged word cannot be published at all, however
legitimate the paper. Where that happened, the citation was dropped and replaced rather than
edited — a doctored title would be worse than no citation.

## Known gap on the published page
