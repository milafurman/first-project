2. The refund policy is being replaced

The published version had two gaps. It required a claim “within a reasonable window of delivery” while the site's FAQ said “within 48 hours” — so a day-four claim satisfied one document and breached the other. And although the footer link is labelled “Shipping & Returns,” the page said nothing about returns at all.

The replacement below closes both, states that products are made to order, requires photographic and video evidence for any damage claim, and confirms delivery by carrier tracking.

Replacement text — validated, not yet published

All orders are packaged and documented for laboratory research use, with certificates of analysis available for verification prior to or upon shipment. Shipping timelines and carrier options are confirmed at checkout.

Delivery is confirmed by carrier tracking. An order the carrier records as delivered counts as received, and carrier tracking is the record TrueMG works from for any question about whether a parcel arrived.

Products are made to order, so TrueMG does not accept returns or issue refunds on intact orders. Once material has left our control, it cannot be re-verified against its certificate or returned to stock.

If your shipment arrives damaged or contains a documented fulfillment error, report it within 48 hours of delivery. Include your order number, photos of the parcel and vial as received, and one continuous, unedited video of the package being opened. The video should start with the sealed parcel, show the shipping label, and show the contents and any damage. Claims without this video cannot be approved. Approved claims are resolved by replacement.

Orders confirmed as inconsistent with research-use terms will not be fulfilled. Contact our support channel for any shipping or order-accuracy concern.

The FAQ is being updated to match

The answer to “What if a vial arrives damaged?” previously asked only for photographs within 48 hours. It will carry the same requirements as the policy — order number, photographs, and the continuous unedited opening video — so a customer reading either document receives the same instruction.

3. Signature on delivery

Not currently available. The store platform's shipping settings contain no signature, signed-for or insurance control. Labels are purchased through Shippo, which does support signature confirmation as a per-shipment option, so the capability exists but has to be enabled there or exposed by the platform.

Until it is enabled, the policy must not promise it. A stated signature requirement that labels do not actually carry is worse than no statement. The carrier-tracking paragraph above is the protection that is genuinely in force today.

4. Outstanding — requires qualified review

The Terms of Service page is the platform's generic boilerplate. It is substantial and better than nothing, but it was not written for a store supplying research compounds.

Worth checking specifically: that the research-use-only restriction is stated as a binding condition of sale rather than a notice; that the buyer's undertaking to handle material in a laboratory setting is enforceable as written; that eligibility and age requirements match what the FAQ promises; that the evidence requirements for a damage claim — including the video — are supportable as a condition of approval; and that the limitation of liability is appropriate to the category.

This is the document a dispute turns on, and it should be reviewed by someone qualified rather than redrafted for convenience.
