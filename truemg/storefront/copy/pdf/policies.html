2. Two problems found in the refund policy

The claim deadline contradicted the FAQ

The policy required a damage or fulfilment claim “within a reasonable window of delivery” and gave no number. The site's FAQ told customers to report damage “within 48 hours of delivery.” A claim made on day four therefore satisfied one document and breached the other.

There was no returns position at all

The footer link is labelled “Shipping & Returns.” The page covered damage in transit and fulfilment error only, and said nothing about an intact order a customer no longer wants. Support had no answer to give.

3. Proposed replacement text

This has been checked against the platform's own compliance validator and accepted without objection. It is not yet published.

All orders are packaged and documented for laboratory research use, with certificates of analysis available for verification prior to or upon shipment. Shipping timelines and carrier options are confirmed at checkout.

Because products are laboratory reagents, TrueMG inspects and verifies orders prior to dispatch. Any report of a damaged shipment or a documented fulfillment error must be submitted within 48 hours of delivery, with your order number and photographs of the parcel and the vial as they arrived. Approved claims are resolved via replacement or credit at TrueMG's discretion.

Returns of intact orders are not accepted. Once material has left our control it cannot be re-verified against its certificate or returned to stock, so an unopened vial cannot be resold as documented research material. This is why the certificate for every lot is published before you order rather than after.

Orders confirmed as inconsistent with research-use terms will not be fulfilled. Contact our support channel for any shipping or order-accuracy concern.

The deadline now matches the FAQ, and the policy carries the FAQ's photograph and order-number requirement, so a customer reading either document receives the same instruction. No change to the FAQ is required.

4. Outstanding — requires qualified review

The Terms of Service page is the platform's generic boilerplate. It is substantial and better than nothing, but it was not written for a store supplying research compounds.

Worth checking specifically: that the research-use-only restriction is stated as a binding condition of sale rather than a notice; that the buyer's undertaking to handle material in a laboratory setting is enforceable as written; that eligibility and age requirements match what the FAQ promises; and that the limitation of liability is appropriate to the category.

This is the document a dispute turns on, and it should be reviewed by someone qualified rather than redrafted for convenience.

Research use only. Not for human consumption. Not for diagnostic or therapeutic use.
