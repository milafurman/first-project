The story behind the name

A slow lizard
in the Arizona
desert

Our mark is a Gila monster. It is on the vial for a reason, and the reason is a method rather than a mascot.

In the 1990s, researchers went looking in a place nobody had thought to look: the venom of the Gila monster, a heavy, slow-moving, famously unhurried lizard native to the American southwest. Out of it they isolated and characterised a peptide. It went on to become one of the most studied molecules in the field.

That is the whole idea, and it is why the lizard is on the vial. Not as a mascot — as a reminder of where the interesting material actually comes from. Careful observation of the overlooked. It is the story behind our name and nothing more than that.

Nobody found it by being clever. They found it by paying close attention to something everyone else had walked past.

The hard part

Documenting it honestly, lot by lot, and putting the certificate where a researcher can read it before ordering rather than after complaining.

What is measured

Identity confirms the material is the compound named on the label. Purity is reported as a number, with the method named on the certificate.

Filed against

The lot number printed on the vial. Not a representative sample from a different batch. The vial in your hand, and the paper that describes it.

What we supply, and what we do not

TrueMG Labs supplies reference-grade compounds to laboratory researchers, as reference material for in-vitro work. That is the entire scope. Nothing here is for human or veterinary use, and nothing on this site should be read as guidance for any use in a person or an animal.

We state that plainly rather than burying it, for the same reason we publish the certificates.
