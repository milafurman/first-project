# About page — draft 2, built on the Gila

**Why this rewrite:** the current About page is well-written and entirely about
documentation. The Gila monster — the mark, the name, the reason any of this
exists — appears nowhere on it. It is FAQ question ten, behind an accordion, at
the bottom of the home page.

The move here is to put the lizard first and then **earn it**: the story is not
decoration, it is a statement of method, and the documentation obsession is the
same discipline applied to a different problem. One idea, not two.

⚠️ **Compliance line held throughout.** The Gila story is provenance for the name
and the mark. Nothing below says or implies what any compound does in a person
or an animal. Exendin-4's history is public scientific record and is described
as history — isolated, characterised, studied. What it became, what it treats,
what it does: not stated, not hinted.

## BODY

```
A slow lizard in the Arizona desert

In the 1990s, researchers went looking in a place nobody had thought to look:
the venom of the Gila monster, a heavy, slow-moving, famously unhurried lizard
native to the American southwest. Out of it they isolated and characterised a
peptide. It went on to become one of the most studied molecules in the field.

Nobody found it by being clever. They found it by paying close attention to
something everyone else had walked past.

That is the whole idea, and it is why the lizard is on the vial. Not as a
mascot — as a reminder of where the interesting material actually comes from.
Careful observation of the overlooked. It is the story behind our name and
nothing more than that.

The same discipline, pointed at a different problem

Attention to the unglamorous is also, as it happens, what this catalogue is
built on.

Anyone can fill a vial. The difficult, unglamorous, expensive part is
documenting it honestly, lot by lot, and putting the certificate where a
researcher can read it before ordering rather than after complaining.

So that is what we do. Every lot is analysed by an independent laboratory with
no commercial stake in the outcome. Identity confirms the material is the
compound named on the label. Purity is reported as a number, with the method
named on the certificate. That certificate is filed against the lot number
printed on the vial, so the document and the material in hand describe the same
production run.

Not a representative sample from a different batch. Not one certificate behind
a dozen lots. The vial in your hand, and the paper that describes it.

Vials are labelled with the compound, the quantity, the lot and the research-use
statement before they leave.

What we supply, and what we do not

TrueMG Labs supplies reference-grade compounds to laboratory researchers, as
reference material for in-vitro work. That is the entire scope. Nothing here is
for human or veterinary use, and nothing on this site should be read as guidance
for any use in a person or an animal.

We think that boundary is worth stating plainly rather than burying, for the
same reason we publish the certificates: a researcher deciding whether to order
should be able to see exactly what they are getting, and exactly what they are
not.
```

# Notes on the choices

**"A slow lizard in the Arizona desert"** as the opening line. It is a strange
first sentence for a compound catalogue, which is the point — it earns a second
sentence. Nobody reads "About TrueMG Labs: we supply reference-grade compounds."

**"Nobody found it by being clever."** The thesis of the page, and the hinge the
rest turns on. It makes the lizard a method rather than a logo.

**"Not as a mascot — as a reminder."** Says out loud what the mark is doing,
which stops a reader wondering why there is a reptile on a research vial.

**"It is the story behind our name and nothing more than that."** Lifted almost
verbatim from the existing FAQ answer, and load-bearing: it is the sentence that
keeps the provenance story from reading as a claim. It should not be cut.

**"The same discipline, pointed at a different problem"** is the join. Without it
the page is a nature anecdote followed by an unrelated paragraph about
paperwork. With it, the documentation obsession becomes the proof that the
opening story is sincere.

**"Anyone can fill a vial"** kept verbatim. It is the strongest line on the
current site.

**The third section is new** and does real work. Stating the limits as a
deliberate choice — the same instinct as publishing the certificates — turns the
required compliance language from a legal footnote into a character note. The
restriction becomes evidence of the brand's own standard rather than a hedge
bolted on at the bottom.

**What is deliberately absent:** what the peptide became, what it is used for,
anything about what any compound does. The history stops at "studied".
