I'm trying to get TrueMG to a particular standard of finish, and my reference is
trynurishlabs.com. So before asking you for anything I took it apart to see what
it actually does. Two findings worth sharing, because they're not what I
expected:

1. There's no clever JavaScript. No GSAP, no Swiper, no AOS, no Lenis, no
   scroll observers — I checked for the globals, not just the script tags. Just
   jQuery. Everything that makes that site feel alive is CSS: about 1,360 rules
   and 12 named keyframe animations, 8 of which were running on screen when I
   measured.

2. They're on WordPress, so they can add a wrapper element whenever an effect
   needs one. I can't add a single element, which means some techniques are
   closed to me no matter which properties you allow.

Then I dry-ran every technique they use through preview_design_change to see
where Lapis actually stands, rather than guessing from your last email. Eleven
rules came back refused. In your own words:

  @keyframes                  "@keyframes is not allowed"
  animation                   "not in the allowed property list"
  filter                      "not in the allowed property list"
  mix-blend-mode              "not in the allowed property list"
  mask-image / -webkit-       "not in the allowed property list"
  will-change                 "not in the allowed property list"
  backface-visibility         "not in the allowed property list"
  position: fixed             "can cover the whole page, its disclaimer included"
  z-index > 9                 "the header, menus and the disclaimer sit above that"
  html / body / main          "cannot be targeted"

So, in order of what it would unlock:

1. THE 10,000 CHARACTER LIMIT. This outranks everything else put together.
   I measured the reference site's actual CSS rather than estimating it:
   311,053 characters across 1,184 readable rules plus its inline blocks —
   and three of its stylesheets were cross-origin so I couldn't read them,
   which makes that a floor, not a total. That is 31 times the budget I have.
   Mine is already at 3,679 characters and one colour change from cramped.

   No property you allow matters if there's nowhere to write it. Ideally:
   let a stylesheet be uploaded as a file, so the ceiling stops being the
   constraint. Failing that, 50,000 characters would at least make a
   designed site possible.

2. @keyframes AND animation. This is where the life comes from, and a
   transition can't substitute — a transition needs a state change to fire, an
   animation runs on its own. Could these be allowed under the same 3-second
   cap you've already applied to transitions, honouring prefers-reduced-motion?

3. A STACKING BAND BELOW THE DISCLAIMER — not position: fixed itself.
   Your reason for refusing fixed and high z-index is that a stylesheet
   mustn't cover the compliance line. That's right and I'm not asking you to
   drop it. What I'd ask instead: reserve a range — say z-index 1 to 900 for
   store styling, with the header, menus and disclaimer pinned above — and
   allow position: fixed only inside the store root, never over the disclaimer
   element. Then I can build a cart drawer or a back-to-top with no route to
   hiding compliance text.

4. filter, mix-blend-mode, mask-image. Each does specific work:
   - filter: drop-shadow() gives a cut-out vial a shadow that follows its
     silhouette instead of its bounding box. I hit this exactly and had to bake
     shadows into the PNGs as a workaround.
   - mix-blend-mode: multiply drops a white-ground product photo onto a tinted
     panel without a halo.
   - mask-image with a gradient is how a section fades out instead of ending in
     a hard line. Four of Nurish's softest edges are exactly this.
   will-change and backface-visibility too, if they're easy — they're only ever
   performance hints.

5. A ROUTE HOOK, AND SECTION CLASSES ON EVERY PAGE. Nothing identifies which
   page I'm on, so any rule hits the whole store. And /about, /contact and
   /lab-results have zero lf-section-* hooks, while /store and /catalog have 35
   and 27. The pattern already exists in your code — those three pages just
   never got it. Something like lf-page-about on the store root would do it.

6. A WAY TO SET THE PAGE BACKGROUND. Since html, body and main can't be
   targeted, I can't change the page's own background at all — so even a
   site-wide dark treatment isn't one rule. A theme colour token for it would
   be enough.

What I'm NOT asking for, so you can see I've checked:

  - offsets: theirs top out at 100px, your cap is 400px. Fine as is.
  - transition duration: theirs run 0.13 to 0.275s, your cap is 3s. Fine.
  - backdrop-filter: theirs is blur(8px), your cap is 40px. Fine.
  - clip-path, grid, flex, @media: all working, no complaints.
