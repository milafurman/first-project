The Gila
Mark.

Only one thing survives from the old logo, and it was never a seahorse. It is a Gila monster — and it is the single most defensible idea this brand owns.

Why the lizard earns its place

In 1992 a Bronx VA endocrinologist named John Eng isolated a peptide from Gila monster venom and called it exendin-4. Human GLP-1 falls apart in the blood in about two minutes, which made it useless. The lizard's version lasted hours.

The reason is the animal itself. A Gila monster eats only a few times a year, so it evolved a hormone patient enough to wring every calorie out of a rare meal. The VA declined to patent the finding. Eng patented it himself, licensed it, and in 2005 it reached the market as Byetta. Every GLP-1 since descends from that lizard.

So the mark is not decoration and not a mascot. It is a citation.

AThe beaded silhouetteRecommended

The skin is the mark.

Top-down silhouette in flat gold, with the tubercular scales knocked out as negative space. A Gila monster is a beaded lizard — the texture is the identifying feature, so the texture does the identifying. Survives to 16px, reads at a glance, one colour, no gradient. Gold once per view, exactly as the system says.

BThe banded specimenMost editorial

Drawn like a field plate.

The same animal carrying its real blotched cross-banding instead of the bead texture. Closer to a scientific illustration than to a logo, which suits a company whose entire pitch is documentation. Costs you legibility below about 28px — the bands close up and it turns into a blob. Would need the silhouette as its small-size fallback.

CThe sealBest on a label

A stamp of record.

The silhouette inside a hairline ring carrying the citation: exendin-4 · 1992. It reads as a certification seal, which is precisely the thing this company sells — the named lab, the printed lot, the attached COA. Strongest use is on the vial label and beside the certificate block, not in the header.

DThe lockupMark + wordmark

With the line it deserves.

Heavy grotesk at weight 900, tracking tightened to −0.045em, gold carried by the MG alone so the accent still appears once. The tagline is already written — it is sitting in your own brand doc.

My call

Direction A for the brand, C on the vial label. A is the only one that holds at favicon size, and a store header needs a mark that survives being small. C is wasted in a header and excellent next to a certificate — it turns the citation into the packaging. B is the most beautiful and the least practical; keep it for the About page, printed large, where it can breathe.

These are vector, so nothing here is a mock-up — whichever you pick ships as-is at any size, and recolours to any token in the system.
