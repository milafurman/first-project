## What was tried first, and why it failed
Hand-authored SVG, three rounds. A full-body top-down lizard read as a newt; a
front-facing head read as a smiley face. Both are recorded failures of approach
rather than of execution: a front view turns any animal into a face, and a
full body loses the animal at small sizes. **Heads in profile are what make
animal marks recognisable.** The generated marks got there in one pass.

## Two things that must happen before this is production artwork

**1. It is raster, and a logo has to be vector.** These are JPEGs. Whatever is
chosen needs redrawing as clean vector paths — the dot field as a real pattern
fill, not pixels — before it goes anywhere near a vial label or a print run.

**2. It needs a reduced small-size variant.** Verified on the size ladder: at
44px it still holds, at 28px the beading closes up, at 16px it is a blob. The
fix is standard practice — a second, simplified cut of the same mark with the
dots dropped and only the silhouette, eye and mouth line kept, swapped in below
about 40px. The brand then has a large mark and a small mark, which is what
every mark this detailed ends up needing.

## Still unresolved
The dot field is finer and more uniform than a real Gila monster's beading,
which is chunkier and arranged in bands. Closer to a halftone than to the
animal. Worth one more pass with an illustrator, or one more generation round.
