## What was tried first, and why it failed
Hand-authored SVG, three rounds. A full-body top-down lizard read as a newt; a
front-facing head read as a smiley face. Both are recorded failures of approach
rather than of execution: a front view turns any animal into a face, and a
full body loses the animal at small sizes. **Heads in profile are what make
animal marks recognisable.** The generated marks got there in one pass.

## Two things that must happen before this is production artwork

**1. It is raster, and a logo has to be vector.** These are JPEGs. Whatever is
chosen needs redrawing as clean vector paths — the dot field as a real pattern
fill, not pixels — before it goes anywhere near a vial label or a print run.

**2. It needs a reduced small-size variant.** Verified on the size ladder: at
44px it still holds, at 28px the beading closes up, at 16px it is a blob. The
fix is standard practice — a second, simplified cut of the same mark with the
dots dropped and only the silhouette, eye and mouth line kept, swapped in below
about 40px. The brand then has a large mark and a small mark, which is what
every mark this detailed ends up needing.

## Still unresolved
The dot field is finer and more uniform than a real Gila monster's beading,
which is chunkier and arranged in bands. Closer to a halftone than to the
animal. Worth one more pass with an illustrator, or one more generation round.

# Regal — and the tradeoff it brings

"Regal" was not solved by adding a crown. It was solved by changing three things:
the **bearing** (chin lifted, head carried high, the composure of a sovereign on
currency), the **beading** (graded jewel-like beads that follow the skull and
rise into a crest along its top, chased goldwork rather than an even halftone),
and the **frame** (two concentric hairlines, the rim of a minted coin).

The coin reading is not decoration here. This company sells certification — the
named lab, the printed lot, the attached certificate. A mark shaped like a
struck coin says *this has been assayed* before a single word is read.

## The tradeoff, stated plainly

The dimensional goldwork that makes it regal is exactly what stops it reducing.
Verified on the ladder: at 120 and 76 it is superb, at 44 the beads begin to
merge, at 28 and 16 it is a gold smudge. Shaded art also cannot be foil-stamped,
embossed, or screen-printed on a vial label, and those are the places this mark
most needs to work.

**So the brand needs two cuts of the same mark, not one:**

| Cut | Where it runs |
|---|---|
| **Full** — shaded, jewelled, framed | Hero, About, packaging art, anything above ~64px |
| **Flat** — one colour, silhouette plus the bead field as a simple pattern | Header, favicon, vial label, foil, embossing, anything below ~64px |

This is ordinary practice for any mark with this much detail, not a compromise.
The flattened reduction is shown on the comparison sheet and reads cleanly.

## Against the locked system
The brand book says stark black and white, gold as the single signal, never a
wash and never soft. A dimensional mark is a departure from the flatness of the
rest of the system — but it is one object, used once per view, in the one place
a brand is allowed a jewel. Recorded as a deliberate exception rather than
slipped through. If it is to be refused, the flat cut already exists and the
system holds.

# Stencil — the original language, which should have been the starting point

`mark-original.png` is the existing mark, isolated from the vial label at full
resolution. **It was never examined before design work started.** It had only
ever been seen at thumbnail size in the header, where it renders dark on dark.
Five rounds of exploration ran without once looking at the thing being kept.
Everything that was wrong with those rounds follows directly from that.

## What the original actually is
A lizard head in strict left profile, drawn as **flat single-colour stencil
artwork**: crisp cut shapes with sharp tapering points, every piece of interior
detail — jaw, nostril, eye, slit pupil — formed by negative space rather than by
outlines. It sits inside a **sweeping open ring** that breaks and tapers from
thick to thin. No shading, no gradients, no texture fill. The energy is esports:
a snarling dragon rather than a composed lizard.

That language is good and it is already the brand's own. Regal did not require
abandoning it; it required raising the chin and calming the crest.

## Why the stencil route beats the jewelled one outright
The regal coin treatment needed two cuts of the mark, because its shading could
not reduce below ~44px and could not be foil-stamped or embossed. **Flat stencil
art has no such problem.** The same artwork runs at 120px and at 16px, in gold
on ink and in one colour on paper, on screen and on a foil-stamped vial label.
One mark, every surface. That is a real advantage, not a consolation.

## The lesson, recorded
Look at what is being kept before designing its replacement. A full-body lizard
read as a newt, a front view read as a smiley face, and a jewelled coin read as
an alien artefact — three failures that one minute spent on the source file at
full size would have prevented.
